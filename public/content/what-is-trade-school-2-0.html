"Trade School 2.0" is Project Arklight's name for a different kind of workforce institution — and the difference is in the incentive, not the branding. This page defines the term and how it works. To see the program itself, visit Trade School 2.0.

What is Trade School 2.0?

It is a software-enabled workforce institution that does three things end to end: assess candidates for aptitude and fit, train them to measured, production-ready competency, and deploy them into real roles. The output is an operator who can do the work on day one — the equivalent of five-plus years on the job — produced in under a year.

How is it different from a traditional trade school?

The moment a traditional trade school plugs into Title IV federal aid, it inherits the university incentive structure: it gets paid to enroll students, so it optimizes for seats filled. Trade School 2.0 flips the objective — it is measured on the operators it produces and deploys, with employers, not teenagers, carrying the risk.

How does it work?

Three stages, all measured. Assess for aptitude and eligibility (including US-Person status for defense work). Train on real production work with competence verified directly rather than by hours logged. Deploy production-ready. Because competence is measured before placement, the operator performs on day one — see measuring competency when the stakes are real.

Who is it for, and what roles does it train?

Two audiences. Companies rebuilding America's industrial base — defense, semiconductors, energy, advanced manufacturing — that can't hire enough skilled operators (see manufacturing workforce development). And candidates who want a high-paying skilled career without a four-year degree. The roles: machinists, metal fabricators and welders, and industrial technicians and operators — the production-floor jobs binding American reindustrialization.

The bottom line

Trade School 2.0 isn't a school attached to a factory — it's a factory with a school inside it, measured on the talent it produces. That single change in incentive is the whole idea. See the program at Trade School 2.0, or partner with us.

About Project Arklight

Project Arklight is a workforce-development company rebuilding how America trains skilled industrial labor.

We run a software-enabled trade school, Trade School 2.0, that assesses, trains, and deploys production-ready operators (electricians, machinists, welders, fabricators) to the companies reshoring American manufacturing. We also publish original research on the skilled-labor gap: where it is, how deep it runs, and what it takes to close it. A shortage of skilled workers is the biggest obstacle to rebuilding American industry, and Project Arklight exists to remove it.
