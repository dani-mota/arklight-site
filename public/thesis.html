The Foundation
01

America's Workforce Arc

1944
GI Bill
8 million veterans trained. Created the American middle class and the most skilled workforce on Earth.
1950s–60s
Vocational Golden Age
National network of trade schools, apprenticeships, and employer-sponsored training. Manufacturing peaks at 19.5M workers.
1970s–80s
The Pivot
"Go to college" becomes national policy. Vocational education defunded and stigmatized. Manufacturing begins offshoring.
2000s
The Hollowing
Manufacturing collapses from 17M to 12M. Defense workforce falls from 3M to 1.1M. Institutional knowledge disappears.
Today
The Crisis
1.9M jobs at risk of going unfilled. Average skilled operator is 63. $3T in reshoring investment with no workforce to execute it.

There is a pattern in the rise and fall of industrial powers. It's not about resources. It's not about geography. It's not about capital.

It's about people.

The United States became the most powerful industrial nation in history not because of its natural resources — other nations had those — but because it built the most sophisticated system for producing skilled, capable people ever created.

Then, starting in the 1970s, America made a choice. It decided that the path to prosperity ran through the university, not the shop floor. Vocational education was defunded. Trade schools were stigmatized. "Go to college" became the only acceptable answer.

The consequences of that choice are now arriving. And they are arriving all at once.

The Crisis
02

How Bad Is It?

Bad. The defense industrial workforce has collapsed by two-thirds. Half the skilled manufacturing workforce will retire within the decade. $3 trillion in reshoring investment is constrained by a single bottleneck: there aren't enough qualified people.

01
National Security

The defense industrial base — the network of companies, workers, and supply chains that produce everything the U.S. military uses — is in structural crisis. Not a funding crisis. A people crisis.

The workforce has collapsed from 3 million workers in the mid-1980s to 1.1 million today. The Navy needs 250,000 new workers this decade. It is not on track to find them.

The Opportunity
04

The Window

Five structural forces are converging simultaneously — for the first time — creating a narrow window to build something that has never existed before.

01
The Workforce Cliff Is Here
Not a projection. 26% of the manufacturing workforce retires by 2030. The defense industrial workforce has already fallen from 3 million to 1.1 million. 495,000 manufacturing jobs are open right now. The knowledge loss is happening in real time.
02
AI Can Finally Encode Human Judgment
For the first time, AI can meaningfully assess cognitive capability, behavioral patterns, and technical aptitude through natural interaction. Voice-first AI can conduct psychometric evaluation that previously required days of in-person observation. The technology to build an intelligent talent system did not exist five years ago. It does now.
03
The Capital Is Waiting for the Workforce
$52B CHIPS Act. $369B IRA. $3T+ reshoring investment. Record defense spending. The capital to rebuild American industry is deployed. It is waiting for workers.
04
Bipartisan Political Consensus
Industrial policy has become one of the few areas of genuine bipartisan agreement. The DoD has invested $130M+ in industrial skills initiatives. Manufacturing USA combines $1B federal + $2B industry investment. This policy environment doesn't depend on which party holds power.
05
The Investor Paradigm Shift
Defense tech VC funding hit $49.1B in 2025 — nearly doubling year-over-year. Hadrian reached $1.6B valuation. Atomic Industries raised $52M. The market has validated AI manufacturing at scale. What hasn't been funded is the talent layer every one of these companies needs.
Whoever builds the talent infrastructure first will define how American industry operates for decades. The window is open. It will not stay open.
The Answer
05

What If the System Was Designed to Actually Work?

What would it look like if someone built a talent infrastructure system from scratch — designed not around institutional convenience, but around the actual requirements of industrial performance?

Closed Loop
Talent
Infrastructure
Diagnose
AI-driven assessment of cognitive capability, behavioral integrity, and technical aptitude
Develop
Training inside live production. Real contracts fulfilled as part of the learning cycle
Deploy
Mission-ready operators placed into roles optimized for their profile, not their resume
Learn
Performance data flows back. Assessment sharpens. The system gets smarter every cycle

Diagnose — An AI system that quantifies cognitive capability, behavioral integrity, and technical aptitude through natural interaction. Built on 80+ years of psychometric research. Every candidate benchmarked against top operators. The system sees potential that credentials obscure.

Develop — Training inside live production. Real manufacturing contracts fulfilled as part of the learning cycle. The diagnostic identifies weaknesses; training prescribes interventions. Four years of capability compressed into one. Every part produced is a lesson.

Deploy — Operators whose readiness has been quantified across every dimension, placed into roles optimized for their profile. Employers receive deployment intelligence: not resumes, but precise assessment of who will perform, where, and why.

Learn — Real-world performance data flows back into the system. Assessment accuracy improves. Training prescriptions sharpen. Deployment matching gets more precise. Every cycle compounds the system's advantage.

No one can replicate this by building a better assessment tool or a better trade school. It requires the integrated system — and the data flywheel that comes from operating it.

I started this company because I saw, from the inside, how the system fails.

I served on a university foundation board. I watched an institution collect tuition from students it was not preparing for the world they would enter. I watched the incentive structure reward enrollment, not outcomes. I watched graduates leave with debt and no applicable capability.

At the same time, I watched the most sophisticated defense and manufacturing companies in America struggle to find the skilled people they needed. Not because capable people don't exist. But because no system exists to find them, develop them, and deploy them.

The talent is there. It's in the veteran who served six years and has no credential to show for the judgment she built. It's in the kid from a rural county who can diagnose a machine by sound but never took the SAT. It's in the career changer who was told manufacturing was a dead end and believed it.

They're invisible to the current system. They shouldn't be.

The nation that builds the best people builds the future. We stopped building them.

We're building the system that starts again.
