# Persona A — Head of Talent

## Surface
- **Titles:** VP of Talent, Head of Talent, VP Talent Acquisition, CPO.
- **Reports to:** COO or CEO.
- **Owns:** Whether the company's growth plan is survivable from a people standpoint.
- **Altitude:** Strategic. Promoted past the req-by-req grind; now accountable for workforce capability as a function.

## What they actually do all day
Their calendar is a collision of two altitudes. Half of it is strategic — workforce planning meetings, headcount forecasts, board-prep decks where they have to defend whether the people plan supports the production plan. The other half is firefighting — a critical req that's been open five months, a hiring manager escalating because a line can't run, an agency relationship that isn't delivering. They live in the gap between what leadership expects and what the market will actually give them. The dominant feeling is that the work is never done: they close one critical role and three more open, and the labor market keeps getting tighter underneath them.

## What they are measured on (external scorecard)
- Whether the workforce plan supports the ramp.
- Quality of hire.
- Early retention — increasingly weighted over raw offer/acceptance counts.
- Ultimately: program-delivery credibility to the government customer.

## What they actually feel (internal scorecard)
This is the layer that moves them, and it rarely appears in a deck.

**Their core fear:** being the person who let the company down on the one thing they own. In a DIB firm the stakes are unusually concrete — if they can't staff the line, a contract slips, and the failure is visible all the way up to the board and out to the government customer. It's not "we missed a hiring target." It's "we couldn't deliver, and it was the people problem, and that was mine."

**The private erosion:** competence anxiety. This is a senior, capable person who is used to solving problems, now facing one that doesn't yield to effort. They can work harder, spend more, add agencies — and the numbers barely move, because the pool doesn't exist. That gap between effort and result is corrosive. Leaders report that the work feeling *unsuccessful* — goals that feel unattainable no matter the hours — is what turns ordinary pressure into something heavier. They are carrying a problem that makes them feel, quietly, like they're failing at the thing they were hired to be good at.

**The isolation:** they often lack a peer who truly gets it. Leadership wants the problem solved and treats it as their job to solve; the team below looks to them for answers they don't have. There's frequently no one at their level to think it through with. This matters enormously for how Arklight should show up — as a peer who understands the problem, not a vendor pitching at them.

**What they want, underneath the KPIs:** to be the person who *solved* it. Not who coped with it, not who managed the shortfall — who found the structural answer and made the ramp staffable. The emotional payload of Arklight's offer isn't "we'll fill your reqs." It's "you can be the leader who fixed the pipeline instead of the one who kept apologizing for it."

## Their inner narrative about the problem
"We can't hire our way out of this. I've known it for a while, but admitting it feels like admitting I can't do my job the normal way. Every option on the table is a version of the same thing — pay more, source harder — and I've watched all of them fail to keep up. Something has to change structurally, but building our own pipeline sounds slow and risky and outside my comfort zone. If I bet on that and it doesn't work, that's on me too."

That last sentence is the real obstacle. This persona's hesitation isn't skepticism that building works — it's fear of owning a second, bigger bet if it fails.

## What earns trust / what breaks it
- **Earns:** Peer-level candor. Evidence over adjectives. Someone who names the problem exactly as they experience it and reduces the *risk* of the build, not just the labor gap. Third-party proof (e.g., military-derived training cutting time-to-productive in half) matters because it de-risks their bet.
- **Breaks:** Hype and credential-theater — they've been burned by agencies promising qualified people and delivering warm bodies. Anything that smells like staffing/placement. Anything that adds risk to a bet they're already afraid to make.

## Sales posture
Must be **convinced that building beats buying** — but the convincing is emotional as much as logical. They likely already believe building is the answer. What they need is the confidence to own the bet. Lead by naming the problem the way they feel it, then de-risk the build.

## To validate
- Which specific fear dominates — delivery failure, personal competence, or career exposure.
- Whether they've already concluded "we must build," or still need that argument.
- The single KPI they're personally judged on.

# Persona B — Director of Workforce Development

## Surface
- **Titles:** Director of Workforce Development, Director of Apprenticeship / Training, Head of Training, Manufacturing Training Manager.
- **Reports to:** VP of Operations, Head of Talent, or COO.
- **Owns:** The pipeline itself — apprenticeships, school partnerships, internal training.
- **Altitude:** Operational builder. Doesn't need convincing that building is right — building *is* the job.

## What they actually do all day
They're hands-in-the-machine. Designing training curricula, managing relationships with community colleges and trade schools, running the apprenticeship cohort, troubleshooting why the last batch of trainees isn't ramping fast enough on the floor. They spend real time with the senior machinists whose knowledge they're trying to capture before those people retire. They're closer to the actual work and the actual trainees than Persona A, and they measure themselves by whether people come out the other end genuinely capable.

## What they are measured on (external scorecard)
- Program throughput — workers produced per cycle.
- Time-to-competency.
- Whether graduates actually stay and perform on the floor.

## What they actually feel (internal scorecard)

**Their core fear:** building a program that produces people who *look* trained but can't actually perform — and being exposed when those graduates hit the floor and struggle. Their credibility is tied to output quality in a way that's very visible. A cohort that washes out is their name on it.

**The private frustration:** they believe in the mission but the tools are inadequate. The traditional method — shadow a senior guy, learn by trial and error — is too slow and too inconsistent, and they know it, but building something better is hard, under-resourced, and slow. They're often trying to construct a rigorous pipeline with duct tape and goodwill while the ramp demand accelerates past them. The gap between the program they *could* build and the one they have the resources to build is their daily friction.

**The race against retirement:** there's a quieter grief and urgency here. They watch decades of irreplaceable tribal knowledge walk out the door with every retirement, and they have no structured way to capture it fast enough. It's a slow-motion loss they feel personally responsible for stemming.

**What they want, underneath the KPIs:** to build the thing that actually works — a real, repeatable system that turns raw hires into capable people at speed, with their name on the design. They have a builder's pride. The emotional payload of Arklight's offer to them isn't "we'll do it for you." It's "we'll give you the system to build it right, faster than you could alone." Arklight equips their ambition rather than replacing it.

## Their inner narrative about the problem
"I know we have to build this ourselves — that part isn't the question. The question is how to do it fast enough and consistently enough to matter. What I've got is too slow. The old shadow-the-senior-guy approach doesn't scale and it's inconsistent, and we're losing the people who hold the knowledge faster than I can capture it. I don't need someone to sell me on training. I need a better method than the one I'm stuck with."

## What earns trust / what breaks it
- **Earns:** Program mechanics that are concrete and fast. Measurable competency outcomes. A method that maps to real production requirements, not generic curriculum. Respect for their expertise — they're a builder, treat them like a collaborator.
- **Breaks:** Anything slow, generic, or academic. Training that doesn't connect to the actual floor. Being talked down to or having their existing belief in building "sold" back to them.

## Sales posture
**Already sold on building; needs a better method.** Skip the "why build" argument entirely — it insults their premise. Lead with speed, competency measurement, and outcomes. Position Arklight as the superior system that makes their build succeed.

## To validate
- Whether they own budget or influence it via Persona A / Ops.
- The specific failure mode they fear most (washout, slowness, inconsistency).
- How they currently measure competency — and where that measure fails them.

# Shared psychology (true of both)

- **Both are exhausted by the vendor field.** The dominant motion aimed at them is "cleared bodies, deployed fast" — staffing agencies that treat symptoms and profit from churn. They've learned to distrust the pitch reflexively.
- **Both operate inside a structurally impossible market.** ITAR-constrained pool, comp war they can't win against the SpaceX/Anduril tier. They *know* the normal playbook can't solve it, which produces a specific psychological readiness for a structural alternative — if it's credible.
- **Both feel the weight of consequence.** In the DIB, a staffing failure isn't an HR miss — it's a delivery failure to the government, with national-security framing. The stakes press on them personally.
- **Both respond to peer-to-peer, evidence-first, no-hype register.** Not a stylistic preference — it's the only register that survives their earned skepticism.
- **Both want to be the protagonist of the solution, not rescued from the problem.** Arklight wins by making them the hero who built/fixed it, never by implying they failed and need saving.

# Appendix — Operationalizing the personas for ATP + GEO content
*(Added 2026-07 to connect the ICP to the search/prompt strategy. This is the layer that turns Answer the Public from a volume tool into a targeting tool.)*

**The governing principle:** these buyers barely search, and when they do the volume is tiny (`hire machinists` ≈ 0/mo, `workforce development` ≈ 50/mo). So we do **not** use ATP to chase volume. We use it to mine the **questions and the exact language** each persona uses around their *problem*, then own those questions as the sole credible source — because a 20-search/month question asked by a Head of Talent right before a vendor evaluation is worth more than 5,000 candidate searches.

Each persona researches the **problem space**, not the solution, and not "buy Arklight." Seed ATP with problem-language, filtered by persona:

## Persona A — Head of Talent (strategic, build-vs-buy, fear-driven)
- **ATP seed language (problem-space):** manufacturing labor shortage; skilled workforce shortage; defense manufacturing workforce; can't hire machinists/welders; cost of unfilled manufacturing jobs; staffing agency vs building talent pipeline; build vs buy workforce; how to solve the manufacturing labor shortage; workforce planning for a production ramp; ITAR cleared workforce shortage; how to staff a manufacturing ramp.
- **Funnel stage:** top (problem-aware, feeding the fear) → mid (evaluating the structural alternative).
- **Emotional payload to hit:** de-risk the bet — "be the leader who fixed the pipeline, not the one apologizing for it."
- **Existing Arklight pages that serve them:** the shortage briefs (`/research/*` = the problem, quantified — feeds the fear with evidence, not hype), `/content/build-buy-rent-industrial-workforce` (their exact dilemma), `/content/clearable-manufacturing-talent` (ITAR), `/content/manufacturing-workforce-development`, `/hire`, `/research/demand-model-dataset`.
- **Likely gaps to build:** a page on the *cost/consequence of not staffing the ramp*; a *how leaders de-risk building a pipeline* piece; a *build-vs-buy* deep dive framed at their altitude.

## Persona B — Director of Workforce Development (operational builder, method + speed)
- **ATP seed language (problem-space):** how to build a manufacturing training program; in-house operator training program; how to measure competency; reduce time to competency; upskill machinists fast; apprenticeship vs in-house training program; capture tribal knowledge from retiring workforce; competency-based training in manufacturing; time-to-competency benchmarks. *(Note: buyers avoid DOL-registered apprenticeships — lead with employer-run "training program" language, not "registered apprenticeship." See `atp-seed-strategy.md`.)*
- **Funnel stage:** mid (already building; hunting a better method).
- **Emotional payload to hit:** "build it right, faster than you could alone" — equip, never replace.
- **Existing Arklight pages that serve them:** `/content/build-manufacturing-apprenticeship-program` (their exact query), `/content/measuring-competency` (their exact query), `/content/manufacturing-workforce-development`.
- **Likely gaps to build:** a dedicated *time-to-competency* page; *capturing tribal knowledge before retirement*; a competency-measurement method deep-dive (ACI).

## How this feeds the loop
1. Seed ATP per persona (above) → harvest **questions** (how/why/what/vs) + the **vocabulary**, not the volume.
2. Filter every question through: *which persona? which funnel stage? which emotional payload?* A raw question list is noise; the personas make it a targeting map.
3. Route each question to: **sharpen an existing page** (answer-shape it in the persona's register) or **build a gap page**.
4. Prioritize by **intent + persona-fit + zero-competition**, never by volume.
5. Write in the **peer, evidence-first, no-hype register** both personas require — the only voice that survives their earned skepticism.
6. Measure with the citation tracker (does an engine quote us on the question?) → visitor-ID/GA4 (did the right company land?) → Notion (did it become a lead?).

**The last-mile input ATP cannot give:** the *verbatim* fear-language from real discovery calls. ATP gets ~70% of the vocabulary; the personas' "To validate" list gets the rest. Feed call transcripts back into this file as they happen.
