The three doors, and why the third one is empty

Every talent leader staffing a ramp has already walked the first two. Here is the honest comparison.

DoorWhat it gives youWhat it costs you
RentStaffing agency or temp labor. Bodies this quarter.Agency markup on every hour, churn before anyone reaches competence, and nobody you get to keep.
WaitCommunity college or a registered apprenticeship. Credentialed people, eventually.Years, not months. DOL registration, mentor hours, and a graduate who still is not production-ready on day one.
BuildOperators trained to your roles and deployed to your floor.You define the roles and the standard. We run the pipeline.

Longer version: build, buy, or rent your industrial workforce.

The first two doors move the same scarce people around. Neither one adds an operator to the market. That is why the price keeps going up and the wait keeps getting longer.

Why the shortage does not fix itself

Because it is structural, and we have measured it. The Arklight Demand Model builds demand from the bottom up and compares it against hire-ready supply rather than assuming the market clears. The result is a gap that training capacity is not closing:

  • Electricians: roughly 10,000 hire-ready completions a year against demand near 97,000. See the briefing.
  • Machinists: about 16,000 hire-ready against roughly 56,000 in annual demand. See the briefing.
  • Metal fabricators: a shortfall near 48,000 a year. See the briefing.

Reshoring, the defense industrial base, and semiconductor fabs are pulling from the same shrinking pool at the same time. The numbers are open and the method is published, so you can check them: download the dataset.

How it works

A pipeline is a repeatable system, not a job posting. Ours has three stages.

  1. Assess. We screen for aptitude and trainability against the specific roles you need staffed, not against a generic credential.
  2. Train. Software-enabled instruction on real production work, measured against competency rather than seat time. This is what compresses the timeline. See how we measure competency.
  3. Deploy. Operators arrive able to do the work, matched to your roles, your certifications, and your site.

"Why train them if they will just leave?"

This is the first question every talent leader asks, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a statistic.

You are not buying a person. You are buying a pipeline. A trained operator can leave; a pipeline that produces the next one cannot. The failure mode you are trying to avoid is not attrition, it is having no replacement when attrition happens, which is exactly the position renting and poaching leave you in.

Two things follow. First, operators trained for your operation, on your equipment and your standards, have a reason to stay that a temp placement never had. Second, when someone does leave, the pipeline is still running. That is the difference between a hire and a system.

Who this is built for

We work with manufacturers where the constraint on output is people rather than orders. In practice that means:

  • US-person and ITAR-controlled production roles, where the hireable pool is already narrow before you filter for skill.
  • Greenfield sites and high-rate manufacturing ramps, where headcount has to arrive on a schedule someone already committed to.
  • Teams whose own job postings say "will train", because experienced operators are not available at the volume required. If you have written that line, you have already reached the same conclusion we did.
  • Certification-bound work, where operators need to arrive ready for the standards your customers audit against.

Frequently asked

How do I staff a new production line quickly?

You have three options: rent operators from a staffing agency, wait for a college or registered apprenticeship to produce them, or have them trained and deployed to you. Project Arklight does the third. We assess, train, and deploy production-ready machinists, fabricators, welders, and technicians directly to your floor on your ramp schedule.

How is this different from a staffing agency?

A staffing agency rents you people who already exist, charges a markup on every hour, and rotates them out before they reach competence. Project Arklight produces new operators and deploys them to you permanently. The agency competes with you for a shrinking pool. We add operators to it.

Do I need to register a DOL apprenticeship to work with Arklight?

No. Registering a Department of Labor apprenticeship means paperwork, mentor-hour requirements, and a multi-year commitment before the first operator reaches your floor. Project Arklight delivers the outcome, production-ready operators trained to a measured standard, without requiring you to stand up and register a program of your own.

Can you supply cleared or ITAR-eligible operators?

Yes. A large share of defense and aerospace production roles are gated on US-person status or clearance eligibility, which narrows an already short pool. We build for that constraint rather than around it. See our briefing on clearable manufacturing talent.

What roles can Project Arklight staff?

Machinists, metal fabricators and welders, electricians, and industrial technicians and operators. These are the production-floor roles binding American reindustrialization, and they are the roles our demand model shows in deepest shortfall.
